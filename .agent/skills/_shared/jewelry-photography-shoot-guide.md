# Jewelry photography shoot guide — categories & angles

Saved 2026-08-25 at the user's request, after a "how many jewelry types are
there and what does a shoot look like for each" question. This is a working
reference for planning/briefing a photoshoot (own product shoots, or
evaluating supplier-provided AliExpress photos against what a complete
gallery should ideally cover) across every jewelry category this shop deals
in. Not tied to any specific Etsy API field — pure photography/production
reference.

## The main jewelry categories

11 core categories:

1. **Rings** (band, statement, wrap/adjustable, stackable sets)
2. **Bracelets & Bangles** (chain, cuff, tennis, charm, wrap)
3. **Necklaces & Pendants**
4. **Earrings** (studs, hoops, drops/dangles, huggies, ear cuffs)
5. **Anklets**
6. **Brooches & Pins**
7. **Cufflinks & Tie Clips**
8. **Body Jewelry** (nose rings, belly rings, septum)
9. **Hair Jewelry** (hairpins, hair chains, headbands, combs)
10. **Charms** (standalone, sold to add to existing bracelets/necklaces)
11. **Jewelry Sets** (matching necklace+earring+ring combos)

## Shoot conventions by category

Every category gets two shoot modes — **Studio** (product-only, clean,
e-commerce-standard) and **Lifestyle** (on-body, styled, aspirational) —
each with its own standard angle list.

### 1. Rings
**Studio:** front-facing hero shot (this is the featured/thumbnail image) →
3/4 angle (depth/side profile) → side profile (flat, band width) → back view
(open-back mechanism, or inside band) → macro on the focal detail (stone
setting, engraving, texture) → on a ring cone/display stand, and separately
laid flat on a textured surface (velvet, stone, wood) → scale reference
(coin, ruler, or open palm).

**Lifestyle:** on a single finger, hand relaxed, neutral background → hand
holding an object (coffee cup, book, flower stem) → multiple rings
stacked/worn together (styling shot) → close-up on hand in soft natural
light, other fingers slightly curled to frame the ring → hand resting on
fabric/hair/textured surface matching the ring's aesthetic.

### 2. Bracelets & Bangles
**Studio:** laid flat in a circle/oval on backdrop → draped over a bracelet
display bust/ramp at an angle → close-up on the clasp/closure → macro on any
charm/link detail/texture → for stacked pieces, one shot separated + one
shot together.

**Lifestyle:** on the wrist, arm relaxed at a slight downward angle (most
flattering) → wrist mid-motion (adjusting hair, holding a phone, resting on
a table edge) → stacked with other bracelets/watches ("arm party" shot) →
close crop on wrist against clothing (sleeve cuff, sweater) for seasonal
context.

### 3. Necklaces & Pendants
**Studio:** flat lay in a natural curve (never a stiff circle) → on a
necklace bust/mannequin form, front-facing → 3/4 angle on the bust (drape) →
macro close-up on pendant/clasp → chain-only close-up showing link
style/thickness.

**Lifestyle:** on neck/collarbone, front-facing, outfit shows the neckline →
3/4 turn of head/shoulders (light catch) → layered with other necklaces
(layering shot) → close crop on collarbone only (very common hero lifestyle
shot for pendants).

### 4. Earrings
**Studio:** pair laid flat, symmetrically arranged (mirror-image) → on an
earring display card/stand → one earring alone in profile (shows drop
length/movement for dangles) → macro on the post/hook/backing hardware
(comfort/allergy-relevant to buyers).

**Lifestyle:** profile shot, hair tucked behind the ear, single earring in
focus → 3/4 face angle (earring + face/hair context) → hair partially down,
grazing the earring (boho/delicate styles) → movement shot (hair flip) for
dangle/chandelier earrings, shows the swing.

### 5. Anklets
**Studio:** flat lay in a gentle curve; on a small display stand; macro on
charm/clasp.
**Lifestyle:** on the ankle, foot slightly elevated (sitting, resting on a
stool), often barefoot or with sandals; beach/summer styling common.

### 6. Brooches & Pins
**Studio:** flat on backdrop; pinned into a fabric swatch (shows how the pin
mechanism sits); macro on the pin clasp/back.
**Lifestyle:** pinned onto a lapel, collar, hat, or bag — shown in the actual
wearing context, since brooches are entirely about placement.

### 7. Cufflinks & Tie Clips
**Studio:** pair laid symmetrically; macro on the back mechanism
(toggle/chain); on a small cufflink stand/box.
**Lifestyle:** on an actual shirt cuff, sleeve slightly rolled; tie clip
shown holding a real tie in place.

### 8. Body Jewelry
**Studio:** macro-dominant — extreme close-up given small size, plain
backdrop, with a ruler/mm scale (sizing is critical for this category).
**Lifestyle:** on the actual body part (nose, belly, septum) — this category
leans heavily lifestyle since studio-only shots communicate fit poorly.

### 9. Hair Jewelry
**Studio:** flat lay; on a mannequin head if available; macro on the
clip/comb mechanism.
**Lifestyle:** in actual hair, styled — a hair chain draped through a braid,
a pin placed in an updo, a headband worn front-on and 3/4.

### 10. Charms
**Studio:** flat lay of the charm alone, plus one shot attached to a
bracelet/chain for scale context; macro on the attachment loop.
**Lifestyle:** shown attached and worn, usually as part of a bracelet
lifestyle shot rather than standalone.

### 11. Jewelry Sets
**Studio:** all pieces laid out together in a coordinated flat lay (hero shot
for sets); each piece also gets its own individual studio shots per its own
category above.
**Lifestyle:** one shot wearing the full set together is mandatory — this is
what actually sells a "set" as a set, not just three separate product
photos.

## Universal rules across every category

- **Lighting**: studio shots use soft, diffused light (softbox/lightbox) to
  avoid harsh glare on metal/stones — a single small accent light is
  sometimes added just to catch sparkle on faceted stones.
- **Background consistency**: keep the same backdrop/surface across a whole
  gallery for one listing (matches this shop's own dark-gothic backdrop
  convention already in use across its jewelry listings).
- **Always include one scale-reference shot** — buyers consistently cite
  "didn't realize how small/large it was" as a top return reason; a coin,
  ruler, or in-palm shot solves this.
- **Order matters**: hero/studio front shot first (rank 1), then a mix of
  studio detail + lifestyle shots after, size chart (if any) at rank 2 —
  matches this project's standing image-order rule (see
  `etsy-seo-standards.md`'s Listing Completeness Gate) for every listing.
